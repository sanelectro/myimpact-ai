# MyImpact — M6.6 Goal Criteria and Evidence-Based Assessment

## Milestone overview

**Milestone:** M6.6  
**Status:** Product decisions finalized; implementation is in progress and
acceptance criteria are not confirmed complete.

M6.6 brings AI-assisted criteria and evidence assessment into the existing
Goals workflow. A separate Impact Analysis experience is planned for M6.7 and
may aggregate validated goal assessments and cross-goal patterns.

Implementation breakdown: [M6.6 Technical Plan](./M6.6-TECHNICAL-PLAN.md).

## Objective

When an employee creates a goal, MyImpact proposes specific, observable
criteria for achieving it. The employee reviews, edits, and approves those
criteria. As work progresses, the employee attaches evidence and MyImpact
proposes which criteria the evidence supports, with rationale, source
references, and uncertainty. Goal detail and the main Goals page use one shared
assessment service and consistent status/count rules.

## Product principles

- **AI-assisted:** AI proposes criteria, evidence matches, progress, and gaps;
  users remain in control of criteria and assessment decisions.
- **Grounded:** Every proposed match or status links to supplied evidence and
  relevant source references.
- **No invented expectations:** Do not invent company or role expectations.
  Ask for clarification or flag uncertainty when a goal is ambiguous.
- **Deterministic aggregation:** The backend calculates counts and any later
  agreed score from persisted assessment decisions; the LLM does not perform
  final arithmetic.
- **No fabricated achievements:** AI suggestions are not completed work and
  are never treated as evidence.
- **One assessment engine:** Goal detail, Goals page, dashboards, and future
  reports reuse shared assessment results.
- **Preserve source records:** Assessments do not rewrite goals, evidence, or
  source documents.

## Initial user journey

1. Employee creates a goal.
2. AI proposes measurable or observable criteria.
3. Employee reviews, edits, and approves the criteria.
4. Employee attaches evidence from an existing or supported source.
5. The system retrieves/extracts available content subject to source
   permissions and makes limitations visible.
6. AI proposes evidence-to-criterion matches and statuses with rationale,
   citations, and uncertainty.
7. The system validates the response and records required user decisions.
8. Goal detail displays criterion statuses, linked evidence, and a goal-level
   summary; the Goals page reuses the same assessment results.

## Criteria

Criteria may describe measurable targets, deliverables, required outcomes,
observable behaviors, capabilities, or results needing supporting measurements.
They are proposals—not authoritative expectations—until the employee approves
them.

Users should be able to add, edit, and remove criteria before relying on them.
Store provenance and versions so criterion edits do not silently change the
meaning of existing assessments. If goal wording is too ambiguous to produce
assessable criteria, ask for clarification or surface the uncertainty instead
of inventing a target.

## Evidence assessment

Reuse the existing evidence records, mappings, ingestion, retrieval, and
source-connection patterns where appropriate. External links must only be
retrieved using authorized credentials and supported APIs. If a source cannot
be accessed, explain the limitation and provide an appropriate manual option.

For each proposed evidence-to-criterion match, the model output should include:

- Criterion and evidence identifiers.
- Proposed status or progress.
- Rationale grounded in the evidence.
- Source references such as a URL and relevant text span.
- A confidence estimate, clearly labeled as an estimate.
- Missing information or contradictory evidence when applicable.

The backend validates all identifiers and authorization before persisting
anything. Treat model output as untrusted input. AI assesses all submitted
evidence regardless of manager-review state. Attaching evidence does not
automatically mean a criterion is met, and an AI conclusion based on unreviewed
evidence must never be presented as manager-verified. Keep AI assessment,
employee confirmation, and manager review distinct. Flag inaccessible,
ambiguous, or contradictory evidence rather than silently treating it as
verified.

## Criterion assessment results and lifecycle

Assessment results describe the criterion outcome:

- `NOT_ASSESSED` — no assessment exists.
- `NOT_STARTED` — no meaningful progress has been identified.
- `IN_PROGRESS` — evidence supports partial progress.
- `MET` — evidence supports completion of the criterion.
- `NEEDS_REVIEW` — evidence is ambiguous, conflicting, or insufficient for a
  reliable conclusion.

Assessment lifecycle is separate from the result:

- `CURRENT` — the assessment matches the current criterion version and
  assessment inputs.
- `STALE` — a material input or source may have changed and reassessment is
  required.
- `OBSOLETE` — the assessment belongs to an older criterion version or has
  been superseded.

`NOT_ASSESSED` and `NOT_STARTED` are distinct. Lack of evidence does not by
itself mean work has not started. When a criterion is materially edited, mark
its previous assessment `OBSOLETE`, retain it for history, and create a new
assessment for the updated criterion version.

## User experience

### Goal detail

Show approved criteria and their statuses, counts such as “1 of 5 criteria
met,” evidence linked to each criterion, AI rationale and source links, missing
evidence or unresolved questions, last-analyzed time, freshness, and an
explicit reanalyze action. Let users correct or challenge an assessment.
Reanalysis must not silently erase prior results or source records.

### Goals page

Summarize the latest valid assessment for each goal and offer an **Analyze
goals** action. Reuse valid results when inputs have not changed; analyze
affected goals when evidence, criteria, or sources change; show progress,
failures, and timestamps; and open each goal for its reasoning and sources.
Avoid unnecessary LLM calls by tracking source versions or fingerprints.

## Scoring and aggregation

Support three criterion types initially:

1. **Binary outcome:** progress is 0% or 100%, based on whether the defined
   completion conditions are met.
2. **Milestone-based outcome:** progress is completed milestone weight divided
   by total milestone weight; milestone weights are equal initially.
3. **Measurable target:** for straightforward increasing targets, calculate
   current measured value relative to the target and cap progress at 100%.
   Other target directions require an explicit formula.

Use equal criterion weights by default. Criterion definitions must specify
completion conditions and, where appropriate, measurable targets or milestones.
The backend calculates progress and aggregate results deterministically; AI
proposes assessments and interprets evidence. Unsupported or ambiguous evidence
must not be converted into an arbitrary percentage.

Keep these metrics separate:

- Number and percentage of criteria met.
- Calculated goal progress.
- Evidence coverage.
- AI assessment confidence.

Do not infer status solely from whether an evidence record exists or from AI
confidence. Avoid double-counting criteria that overlap with milestones.

## Architecture and freshness

- `myimpact-ai`: API orchestration, AI workflows, retrieval, assessment,
  deterministic aggregation, and versioning.
- `myimpact-web`: goal creation, criterion review, evidence attachment,
  assessment details, reanalysis, and Goals-page summaries.
- `myimpact-db`: goals, criteria, evidence references, results, source versions,
  user decisions, and audit history.

Reuse existing services and models where appropriate; do not add a separate
microservice for each AI step without a demonstrated need. Every assessment
references the exact criterion version it evaluated. Material changes to
criterion scope, completion conditions, target, milestones, or weights trigger
reassessment; only assessments for the current criterion version contribute to
current goal summaries. Until reassessment finishes, show the current result
as pending or unavailable rather than presenting an obsolete result as current.

Track source identity, version or modification metadata, retrieval time, and
accessibility wherever supported. Re-fetch sources during user-triggered
reanalysis. Background webhooks, connector events, and scheduled freshness
checks are not required for M6.6. When refreshed source content differs, mark
affected assessments `STALE`; flag inaccessible sources for review. Reassess
only affected criteria and dependent summaries. A temporary connector failure
does not invalidate an underlying achievement. Failed retrieval or AI calls
must be visible and must not appear as successful fresh assessments.

## Access and trust boundaries

- Enforce authorization in the backend for goals, evidence, and assessments.
- Use source-system permissions for external integrations.
- Keep manager evidence review separate from AI assessment and user
  confirmation; manager access must not expose private reports or notes.
- Distinguish AI proposals from source facts and user-authored content.
- Audit important assessment decisions and changes.

## Assessment history and AI quality evaluation

Maintain immutable assessment history with:

- Goal and criterion identifiers and criterion version.
- Evidence references and available source versions.
- Assessment result, progress, rationale, and supporting citations.
- Assessment timestamp, model version, and prompt or configuration version.
- Employee corrections and manager-review actions where applicable.
- Invalidation or supersession reason.

Invalidate or reassess when a criterion is materially edited, relevant evidence
is added, removed, or materially changed, an external source changes or becomes
inaccessible, assessment logic/prompts/models change materially under the
regression policy, or the employee requests reanalysis. Use input fingerprints
to target affected assessments and avoid unnecessary processing. Preserve prior
results rather than overwriting history.

Evaluate evidence-to-criterion matching precision and recall, agreement with
human-reviewed outcomes, source grounding and citation validity, unsupported
achievement claims, source-change detection and invalidation reliability, and
consistency across repeated or equivalent inputs. Establish numeric release
thresholds from a representative human-reviewed dataset before production.
Prioritize grounded conclusions and a low unsupported-claim rate over coverage;
the AI must abstain and request review when evidence is insufficient.

## Implementation sequence

1. Audit existing goal, evidence, ingestion, retrieval, and assessment models
   and tests.
2. Define versioned criteria for binary outcomes, milestone-based outcomes,
   and measurable targets; specify completion conditions and applicable
   formulas.
3. Implement AI assessment of all submitted evidence, preserving evidence
   review state, source validity, rationale, citations, uncertainty, and
   confidence.
4. Implement separate criterion-result and assessment-lifecycle states,
   immutable assessment history, employee correction/confirmation, and
   manager-review records.
5. Implement deterministic progress and goal aggregation with equal milestone
   and criterion weights by default.
6. Add Goal detail criteria, evidence, rationale, freshness, status history,
   and reanalysis.
7. Add Goals-page analysis and summaries using the same service.
8. Implement targeted invalidation and source freshness tracking; evaluate
   quality against a human-reviewed dataset and set numeric release thresholds.
9. Harden authorization, failure behavior, and end-to-end consistency.

Each phase needs independently testable acceptance criteria. Do not mark M6.6
complete until the workflow is validated end to end.

## Finalized decisions

### 1. Evidence eligibility and review

AI assesses all submitted evidence, regardless of whether it is unreviewed or
manager-accepted. The assessment indicates the evidence review state and source
validity. AI conclusions based on unreviewed evidence are not manager-verified
achievements. AI assessment, employee confirmation, and manager review remain
distinct. Inaccessible, ambiguous, or contradictory evidence is flagged rather
than silently treated as verified.

### 2. Criterion results, lifecycle, and transitions

Use the result states and separate lifecycle states defined above. A lack of
evidence does not automatically mean work has not started. Material criterion
edits obsolete the previous assessment and trigger a new assessment against
the updated criterion version; preserve the previous result and its evidence
references for audit.

### 3. Employee confirmation

Employee confirmation is optional for routine assessments, not mandatory for
every AI proposal. Present supporting evidence, rationale, and limitations.
Employees can confirm, correct, or challenge an assessment. Request
clarification when evidence is ambiguous, conflicting, or insufficient.
Record corrections without overwriting the AI assessment, and distinguish
AI-assessed, employee-confirmed, and manager-reviewed results. Formal
performance-review workflows may impose stricter requirements later. AI
confidence alone is not proof of achievement.

### 4. Criterion types and progress formulas

Initially support binary outcomes, milestone-based outcomes with equal
milestone weights, and measurable targets with capped progress for straightforward
increasing targets. Other target directions need an explicit formula. Use equal
criterion weights by default. Keep criteria-met counts/percentages, calculated
goal progress, evidence coverage, and AI confidence distinct. AI proposes and
interprets; deterministic backend logic calculates progress and aggregate
results. Do not assign arbitrary percentages to unsupported or ambiguous
evidence.

### 5. Criterion versioning and reassessment

Every material edit to scope, completion conditions, target, milestones, or
weights triggers reassessment. Each assessment references the exact criterion
version evaluated. Mark the previous assessment `OBSOLETE`; preserve its result
and evidence references. Until reassessment completes, show the result as
pending or unavailable. Only results for the current criterion version
contribute to current goal summaries.

### 6. Source freshness and accessibility

Track source identity, version/modification metadata, retrieval time, and
accessibility where supported. Re-fetch on reanalysis; use webhooks/connector
events where available and scheduled checks otherwise. Mark affected
assessments `STALE` when relevant content changes, flag inaccessible sources
for review, and reassess only affected criteria and dependent summaries.
Preserve last-verified time and explain access limitations. A temporary
connector failure does not invalidate the underlying achievement.

### 7. History, invalidation, and AI quality

Keep immutable history containing goal/criterion IDs and version, evidence and
source versions, result/progress/rationale/citations, timestamp, model and
prompt/configuration versions, employee corrections, manager-review actions,
and invalidation/supersession reason. Trigger targeted reassessment after
material criterion or evidence changes, source changes/access loss, material
assessment logic/model changes under the regression policy, or an employee
request. Use input fingerprints to avoid unnecessary reprocessing.

Measure matching precision/recall, agreement with human-reviewed outcomes,
grounding/citation validity, unsupported achievement claims, source-change and
invalidation reliability, and consistency across equivalent inputs. Set numeric
production release thresholds using a representative human-reviewed dataset.
Prioritize grounded conclusions and a low unsupported-claim rate; allow the
model to abstain and request review when evidence is insufficient.

## Deferred

- Impact Analysis (M6.7) aggregates goal assessments and cross-goal patterns
  without independently changing criterion-level assessment records.
- Quarterly/yearly Impact Analysis.
- 1:1 briefs and career reports (M6.8).
- Autonomous performance ratings or promotion decisions.
- Treating AI-generated suggestions as completed work.
- Separate dashboard scoring logic.
- Broad multi-agent orchestration without a concrete need.

## Definition of done

An employee can create a goal, review AI-generated criteria, attach supported
evidence, and inspect traceable AI assessments against each criterion. The
workflow distinguishes criterion results from lifecycle state, AI assessment
from employee confirmation and manager review, and goal progress from evidence
coverage and confidence. Goal detail and the Goals page use the same assessment
engine and deterministic progress/count rules. History is immutable, stale and
obsolete results are explicit, access controls are enforced, and backend, UI,
integration, and AI-evaluation tests meet the numeric release thresholds
established from the human-reviewed dataset.
