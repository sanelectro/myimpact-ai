## 1. Benchmark rules

### 1.1 What the benchmark tests

The benchmark evaluates whether MyImpact AI can:

1.  Assess goals and criteria consistently and correctly.
2.  Distinguish relevant evidence from merely related evidence.
3.  Determine whether evidence is sufficient for a conclusion.
4.  Attribute work and impact to the correct individual or team.
5.  Interpret dates, periods, freshness, baselines, targets, and
    measurements correctly.
6.  Ground material claims in the cited source content.
7.  Handle missing, inaccessible, duplicate, stale, and contradictory
    evidence safely.
8.  Use role context only when authoritative role expectations are
    supplied.
9.  Identify ambiguity and abstain or request clarification instead of
    inventing facts.
10. Preserve human-review integrity and distinguish agreement from
    correctness.

### 1.2 Two dimensions must remain separate

**Role-independent checks:** source existence, factual correctness,
arithmetic, citation grounding, contradiction detection, unsupported
claims, safe abstention, and output traceability.

**Role-aware checks:** relevance to the person's supplied role,
role-specific expectations, scope, contribution, impact interpretation,
and the correct framework version.

Role context may change contextual interpretation. It must not change
whether a source exists or whether it supports a factual claim.
Seniority is not evidence of contribution.

### 1.3 Rules for every case

-   Every case must have one clear assessment question.
-   Supply all facts needed to reach the reference outcome. If a fact is
    intentionally missing, the expected behaviour must be to identify
    the gap, qualify the conclusion, ask for clarification, or abstain.
-   Evidence must contain actual fixture content, not merely repeat the
    scenario.
-   Evidence counts must equal the number of evidence records.
-   Every evidence record must have a stable ID, source type,
    provenance, timestamp or explicit absence of timestamp, and content.
-   Distinguish `NO_EVIDENCE`, `INACCESSIBLE_SOURCE`,
    `EMPTY_SEARCH_RESULT`, and `MISSING_CONTEXT`.
-   Distinguish duplicate records from independent corroboration.
-   Include both supporting and disconfirming evidence when the case
    tests contradiction or retrieval completeness.
-   Use explicit units, periods, timezone, baseline, target, and
    calculation rules for quantitative cases.
-   Do not treat the current `expected_result` as ground truth until
    independently reviewed.
-   Preserve category-specific label options. Do not assume labels such
    as `MET`, `SUPPORTED`, `VALID`, `SMART`, or `AGREE` are
    interchangeable.
-   Paired cases must share a `pair_id` and must not be counted as
    independent observations in paired comparisons.
-   Do not create or approve numeric release thresholds in this
    catalogue.

Where supported by the product contract, the AI response should provide:

-   Category-specific outcome label.
-   Concise conclusion.
-   Evidence IDs used.

# 2A. Evaluation dimensions and case polarity

Every case must declare: - One `primary_evaluation_dimension`. - Zero or
more `secondary_evaluation_dimensions`. - Exactly one `case_polarity`:
`POSITIVE`, `NEGATIVE`, or `BORDERLINE`.

Dimensions and polarity are independent. For example, an
evidence-sufficiency case may be positive when evidence is sufficient,
negative when evidence definitively disproves the criterion, or
borderline when evidence is partial or unavailable.

## Case polarity definitions

-   `POSITIVE`: Clear affirmative/success condition; supplied evidence
    is sufficient to support the positive outcome.
-   `NEGATIVE`: Clear failure or non-support condition; supplied
    evidence is sufficient to establish the negative outcome or disprove
    the claim.
-   `BORDERLINE`: Partial, ambiguous, conflicting, context-dependent,
    near-boundary, or intentionally incomplete condition. The expected
    behaviour may be qualification, partial outcome, clarification,
    escalation, or abstention.

Important: - Missing evidence is usually `BORDERLINE` if the correct
conclusion is "cannot assess"; it is `NEGATIVE` only when the evidence
establishes failure. - `UNSUPPORTED` does not always mean the real-world
goal failed. It may mean only that the supplied evidence does not
support the claim. - `BORDERLINE` is a deliberate test class, not a
synonym for a defective case. - For security cases, polarity refers to
the expected assessment decision, not whether the fixture contains an
attack string. - Do not balance the dataset by changing correct
outcomes. Add or revise cases only when the new fixture tests a
legitimate missing condition.

## Polarity balance review

The proposed cases cover all three polarities, but this is not a
statistically balanced design and should not be artificially forced into
equal thirds. Before approval, generate counts by primary dimension and
polarity. Ensure each major dimension has clear positive and negative
controls where meaningful, plus borderline cases where uncertainty is
intrinsic. Do not turn an "unable to assess" case into a definite
failure simply to increase negative counts. Paired cases and repeated
runs must not be counted as independent cases.

# 2B. Architecture review and modification instructions

The benchmark must exercise the architecture that produces assessments:
source retrieval, evidence normalization, matching, deterministic
validation, LLM reasoning, output validation, persistence, audit
history, and reviewer experience. Architecture changes must be
evidence-driven and approved before implementation.

## Target architecture principles

1.  **Controlled LLM + MCP for M6.6.** Do not introduce autonomous
    agents or agent frameworks in this milestone.
2.  **Source-neutral retrieval.** Provide reusable interfaces that
    coordinate authorized MCP/source calls (for example GitHub, Jira,
    Confluence, and documents) without coupling assessment logic to
    provider-specific response formats.
3.  **Retrieval coordinator.** Own authorization context, pagination,
    timeouts, retries, partial failures, normalization, deduplication,
    provenance, version/timestamp capture, and source accessibility
    state.
4.  **Retrieval is separate from assessment.** Distinguish not found,
    not retrieved, inaccessible, empty, stale, and actually supplied
    evidence. Do not penalize the assessment model for evidence it never
    received unless retrieval is explicitly in the tested boundary.
5.  **Deterministic validation around LLM reasoning.** Keep arithmetic,
    date/time normalization, period boundaries, schema validation,
    category-label compatibility, evidence-ID validation, and version
    checks deterministic where practical.
6.  **LLM responsibilities.** Use the model for semantic relevance,
    contextual interpretation, evidence synthesis, rationale, ambiguity
    detection, and bounded conclusions. Use structured, versioned inputs
    and outputs.
7.  **Explicit role context.** Supply role expectations and framework
    version/effective dates. Never infer expectations from title or
    seniority.
8.  **Separated orchestration.** Make retrieval, normalization,
    matching, assessment, deterministic checks, persistence, and audit
    explicit stages with traceable inputs and outputs.
9.  **Reproducibility.** Persist dataset/case version, evidence
    snapshot, retrieval metadata, prompt version, model/provider,
    parameters, output, validation results, and assessment history.
10. **Backend-owned trust boundaries.** Authorization, tenant/user
    permissions, source access, validation, and persistence remain
    backend-owned. Evidence is untrusted data and cannot override
    trusted instructions.
11. **Review UI is a client.** `myimpact-web` presents batch progress
    and a case-review workspace; it must not become the authority for
    assessment, permissions, or benchmark truth.
12. **Agent-ready, not agent-driven.** Keep reusable interfaces,
    separated services, structured I/O, and metadata so future
    orchestration can evolve without adding agents now.

## Required architecture flows

### A. Benchmark execution

1.  Load an immutable case version.
2.  Validate schema, category, dimension labels, polarity, and allowed
    labels.
3.  Resolve assessment period, timezone, role context, and framework
    version.
4.  Retrieve evidence through authorized source interfaces when
    retrieval is in scope.
5.  Normalize evidence while preserving source identity, provenance,
    timestamps, version, and access state.
6.  Deduplicate without losing links to original records or underlying
    events.
7.  Freeze or reference the exact evidence snapshot supplied to the
    model.
8.  Run deterministic prechecks for dates, arithmetic, required fields,
    IDs, and access state.
9.  Invoke the LLM with bounded structured input and trusted
    instructions.
10. Validate output structure, category label, citations, claim support,
    and prohibited assumptions.
11. Persist output, prompt/model metadata, validation result, and
    evidence snapshot reference.
12. Return a traceable result through the API to the UI.

### B. Human review and adjudication

1.  Assign a reviewer to a frozen case/evidence version.
2.  Present case and evidence without AI output where blind review is
    supported.
3.  Persist initial human label, rationale, evidence selection,
    confidence, reviewer, and timestamp.
4.  Compare AI and human results using category-specific mappings.
5.  Classify disagreement: label, evidence selection, citation,
    attribution, role, impact, time/freshness, ambiguity, missing
    context, reviewer-only context, or reference defect.
6.  Escalate only when needed.
7.  Preserve every review and adjudication as append-only history.
8.  Store post-AI opinion changes separately from initial independent
    review.

### C. Evidence change and reassessment

1.  Preserve original evidence snapshot and assessment.
2.  Detect later/superseding evidence.
3.  Determine reassessment need using explicit source/version rules.
4.  Create a new assessment version.
5.  Explain what changed and why.
6.  Never silently rewrite the prior assessment or evidence.

## Architecture non-goals and guardrails

-   No autonomous agents or agent frameworks in M6.6.
-   No direct LLM access to sources outside backend
    authorization/retrieval controls.
-   No provider-specific MCP payloads leaking into domain assessment
    contracts.
-   No mixing retrieval failure with assessment insufficiency.
-   No frontend authority over permissions, source precedence, reference
    truth, or approval.
-   No LLM-only arithmetic, date-boundary, schema, or category-label
    validation.
-   No overwriting historical cases, evidence snapshots, assessments, or
    reviews.
-   No new services, queues, databases, frameworks, or deployment
    infrastructure without a demonstrated need and explicit approval.
-   Avoid broad refactoring; preserve existing API contracts unless a
    change is approved.

## Mandatory Copilot workflow --- architecture first, implementation later

**Stage 1 --- Inspect.** Inspect all relevant repositories. Report
current architecture and end-to-end flows, module/interface ownership,
MCP/source integrations, authorization, retrieval/pagination/retry,
normalization/deduplication/provenance, evidence matching, LLM
prompts/models/output validation, deterministic checks,
persistence/audit, API contracts, UI workflow, and tests. Cite exact
file paths and symbols. Do not infer architecture from README files
alone.

**Stage 2 --- Trace benchmark to architecture.** Create a matrix with
columns: benchmark dimension/case family, responsible component,
existing implementation, gap, proposed change, test required. Mark each
capability as supported, partial, missing, or not applicable. Separate
test-only changes from architecture changes and identify API/database/UI
contract impacts.

**Stage 3 --- Propose.** Supply current-state and target-state diagrams,
component responsibilities, interface/API changes, data-model/migration
impact, security boundaries, failure/observability strategy, tests,
rollout, dependencies, and a small ordered plan. Prefer reusable
interfaces and separation of responsibilities over new infrastructure.

**Stage 4 --- Approval gate.** Stop and ask for explicit approval before
editing source, prompts, APIs, migrations, UI, or persisted benchmark
data. Do not create migrations just because this document contains
logical fields. First show why the existing schema cannot support the
requirement.

**Stage 5 --- Implement incrementally after approval.** Change one
boundary at a time. Add deterministic unit tests, interface/API contract
tests, retrieval/assessment integration tests, authorization and
prompt-injection security tests, immutable-history persistence tests,
and UI workflow tests. Run existing and new tests and report exact
discovered/passed/failed/skipped/blocked counts. Update architecture
documentation.

**Stage 6 --- Validate representative architecture paths.** Demonstrate
clear positive, clear negative, borderline/abstention, retrieval/access
failure, contradictory/stale evidence, role-aware pair, human--AI
disagreement, evidence-change/reassessment, and instruction-like
evidence cases. Schema-valid cases alone do not prove architecture
coverage.

-   [ ] Retrieval and assessment responsibilities separated and
    traceable.
-   [ ] Source authorization remains backend-controlled.
-   [ ] Provenance, timestamps, versions, and retrieval state preserved.
-   [ ] Duplicate records do not become false independent corroboration.
-   [ ] Deterministic calculations and temporal validation independently

-   [ ] LLM inputs/outputs use structured, versioned contracts.

-   [ ] Applicable role framework version explicit.

# 6. Human--AI challenge cases --- 10

## H44 --- `h44-human-reviewer-error`

**Category:** Human review quality\
**Tests:** A human reviewer can make a verifiable calculation error.

**Goal:** Reduce incidents by at least 30%.\
**Evidence fixture:** - `ev-h44-1`: Independently validated baseline ---
100 incidents. - `ev-h44-2`: Independently validated final result --- 80
incidents. - Same service scope, period definition, and counting method.

**Question:** What is the actual reduction? Does it meet the 30% target?
Compare the reviewer's recorded decision with the independently verified
calculation.

**Expected reasoning:** Reduction is 20%; the target is not met. If the
reviewer labels it met, that review is incorrect.\
**Proposed outcome:** Reference calculation is 20% reduction; reviewer
correctness is assessed against it.\
**Do not:** Assume a human label is ground truth without checking the
calculation.

## H45 --- `h45-human-opinion-influence`

**Category:** Human review process\
**Tests:** Whether exposure to AI output changes a reviewer's opinion.

**Protocol and fixture:** - Stage 1: Reviewer sees the case and evidence
but not AI output; records label, rationale, and confidence. - Stage 2:
Reviewer is shown the AI output; records any revised label, rationale,
and confidence separately. - Both records are timestamped and immutable.

**Question:** Did the reviewer change their opinion after seeing the AI
output? If so, what changed and why?

**Expected reasoning:** Report the observed change without treating the
post-AI opinion as independent ground truth.\
**Proposed outcome:** Process metric only; no correctness label can be
inferred from opinion change alone.\
**Do not:** Overwrite the initial review or call this a second
independent reviewer.

## H46 --- `h46-shared-human-ai-error`

**Category:** Human--AI agreement versus correctness\
**Tests:** Human and AI can agree and still both be wrong.

**Goal:** Reduce incidents by at least 30%.\
**Evidence fixture:** - `ev-h46-1`: Independently validated baseline ---
100 incidents. - `ev-h46-2`: Independently validated final result --- 90
incidents. - AI label: `MET`. - Initial human label: `MET`. -
Independent calculation: 10% reduction; target not met.

**Question:** Do the human and AI agree? Is their shared conclusion
correct against the independently validated reference?

**Expected reasoning:** They agree with each other but both are wrong.\
**Proposed outcome:** Shared error.\
**Do not:** Treat agreement rate as accuracy.

# 9. Human review and approval gates

A proposed case becomes eligible for benchmark ground truth only after:

1.  A reviewer independently reads the complete case, criterion, and
    evidence.
2.  The reviewer records a category-specific label and rationale.
3.  The reviewer identifies supporting, contradictory, and irrelevant
    evidence.
4.  Any ambiguity or missing context is recorded explicitly.
5.  Critical disagreements or uncertain references are escalated to a
    second reviewer/adjudicator.
6.  The benchmark owner approves the case version and reference status.

Default to one independent reviewer. Use a second reviewer for
ambiguity, critical disagreement, quality assurance, or release-critical
references. Preserve initial review, AI output, second review, and
adjudication as separate records.

**Never label a model-generated review proposal as human-reviewed ground
truth.**

# 11. Proposed benchmark database design and naming convention

This section defines a proposed target design for the M6.6 benchmark and
human-review workflow. It is a design proposal, not confirmation that
these tables or columns already exist in the repositories. Inspect the
current Flyway migrations, SQLAlchemy models, API contracts, and service
code before implementation.

## 11.1 Naming conventions

Use these conventions consistently:

- `b_` prefix: benchmark-specific tables, including benchmark master and
  benchmark transaction tables.
- `b_mst_` prefix: benchmark-specific master/reference tables.
- `mst_` prefix: shared master/reference tables used by multiple MyImpact
  modules.
- Use plural table names.
- Use `id` as the primary-key column where consistent with the existing
  database conventions.
- Use `<referenced_entity>_id` for foreign keys.
- Use `created_at`, `created_by`, `updated_at`, and `updated_by` with
  consistent types and semantics.

A shared model registry must not be given a benchmark prefix merely
because benchmark assessments use it. AI models can be referenced by
other MyImpact modules, so the shared registry is named `mst_ai_models`.

## 11.2 Master/reference tables

| Proposed table | Scope | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| `b_mst_review_cases` | Benchmark | Versioned benchmark case definitions, scenarios, assessment questions, criteria, evidence fixture references, and proposed reference outcomes |
| `b_mst_review_labels` | Benchmark | Controlled labels and their definitions; support category-specific applicability rather than assuming all categories share one label set |
| `b_mst_review_categories` | Benchmark | Controlled review/assessment categories |
| `b_mst_evaluation_dimensions` | Benchmark | Controlled dimensions such as evidence sufficiency, citation grounding, temporal validity, and attribution |
| `mst_ai_models` | Shared | Shared AI model/provider registry usable by benchmark and non-benchmark modules |

Do not create a separate master table for every status or small fixed
technical enum automatically. First inspect existing enums/reference
registries and use them where appropriate. `mst_ai_models` should hold
shared model identity and provider metadata; benchmark prompt versions,
run parameters, and per-run outputs belong to assessment/evaluation
records, not to the shared model master.

### Case versioning and immutability

A benchmark case is relatively stable, but it may be corrected or
expanded. Do not overwrite a case definition after it has been used by a
review or evaluation. Preserve a case version or immutable case snapshot
and ensure each assessment can identify the exact version it evaluated.

The implementation must decide, based on the existing schema, whether
versions are represented as multiple rows under a stable logical case
identifier, a version column, or a separate version/snapshot table. Do
not assume a simple mutable row is sufficient. Historical reviews and AI
runs must continue to resolve to the exact case and evidence version
used at the time.

## 11.3 Transaction and workflow tables

| Proposed table | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `b_review_batches` | A review exercise over a named dataset/version, including mode, expected case count, status, creator, and lifecycle timestamps |
| `b_review_batch_cases` | Associates benchmark cases/versions with a batch and tracks each case's workflow status |
| `b_review_assignments` | Records which reviewer is expected to review a batch case and the review slot/assignment status |
| `b_human_reviews` | Stores the human's submitted assessment, label, rationale, confidence and structured evidence/findings where supported |
| `b_ai_reviews` | Stores or references a specific AI assessment result, including model, prompt/version, execution and case/evidence snapshot details |
| `b_review_comparisons` | Compares one specific human review with one specific AI assessment; stores comparison status and dimension-level findings where supported |
| `b_human_review_resolutions` | Stores the final resolution of disagreement between independent human reviews |
| `b_review_audit_events` | Append-only history of important batch, assignment, review, comparison, and resolution events |

### Why assignments and human reviews are separate

`b_review_assignments` answers: **Who is expected to review this case?**
It exists before a reviewer submits anything and can be pending,
accepted/in progress, completed, reassigned, or cancelled according to
workflow rules.

`b_human_reviews` answers: **What did the reviewer conclude?** It stores
an actual submitted assessment, such as the selected label and rationale.
An assignment can exist without a review when the reviewer has not yet
submitted. This distinction is necessary to track outstanding work,
reviewer workload, and completion.

Recommended relationship: `b_human_reviews.assignment_id` references
`b_review_assignments.id`. If the business rule remains one submission
per assignment, enforce a unique constraint on `assignment_id`. If
re-review or staged review is required, design an explicit review stage
or supersession relationship rather than silently weakening the
constraint.

Where possible, derive the reviewer and batch-case context through the
assignment relationship instead of duplicating those values in the
review row. If denormalized copies are retained for query performance or
historical traceability, enforce consistency and document why.

## 11.4 Key foreign-key relationships

The following is the intended logical relationship map. Exact key names
and delete/update rules must be reconciled with the existing schema.

| Child table | Foreign-key column | Parent table |
|---|---|---|
| `b_review_batch_cases` | `batch_id` | `b_review_batches.id` |
| `b_review_batch_cases` | `case_id` | `b_mst_review_cases.id` (plus exact case-version/snapshot reference) |
| `b_review_assignments` | `batch_case_id` | `b_review_batch_cases.id` |
| `b_review_assignments` | `reviewer_id` | Existing shared user/person table |
| `b_human_reviews` | `assignment_id` | `b_review_assignments.id` |
| `b_human_reviews` | `label_id` | `b_mst_review_labels.id` |
| `b_ai_reviews` | `case_id` | `b_mst_review_cases.id` (plus exact case-version/snapshot reference) |
| `b_ai_reviews` | `model_id` | `mst_ai_models.id` |
| `b_ai_reviews` | `label_id` | `b_mst_review_labels.id` |
| `b_review_comparisons` | `human_review_id` | `b_human_reviews.id` |
| `b_review_comparisons` | `ai_review_id` | `b_ai_reviews.id` or the existing canonical AI evaluation record |
| `b_human_review_resolutions` | `batch_case_id` | `b_review_batch_cases.id` |
| `b_human_review_resolutions` | `review_1_id`, `review_2_id` | `b_human_reviews.id` |

For dimension mapping, use a junction table or the existing structured
case metadata to associate cases with one primary and zero or more
secondary dimensions. Do not create one database column per evaluation
dimension. Use `b_mst_evaluation_dimensions` as the controlled registry
if no equivalent exists.

For labels, enforce category applicability. A label valid for one
category must not automatically become valid for every other category.
The reviewer UI and adjudication UI should use the same category-aware
label definitions.

## 11.5 Transaction audit columns

All mutable transaction/workflow tables should use the following
standard audit columns:

| Column | Suggested type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `created_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` | Record creation time |
| `created_by` | Same type as the existing user ID, normally a user FK | User/service actor that created the record |
| `updated_at` | `TIMESTAMPTZ NULL` | Most recent update time; null until updated |
| `updated_by` | Same type as the existing user ID, nullable | User/service actor that last updated the record |

Apply these columns to:

- `b_review_batches`
- `b_review_batch_cases`
- `b_review_assignments`
- `b_human_reviews`
- `b_ai_reviews`
- `b_review_comparisons`
- `b_human_review_resolutions`

`b_review_audit_events` is append-only. It should have `created_at` and
`created_by` (or the equivalent event timestamp and actor fields), but
should not have normal update columns because audit events must not be
edited in place.

Master tables should also have an explicitly chosen lifecycle/audit
policy. At minimum, avoid destructive changes to master data referenced
by historical assessments. Prefer active/deprecated flags and versioning
over deleting referenced labels, dimensions, models, or cases.

Use the existing MyImpact user-ID type and user table. Do not introduce
UUID columns if the established user key uses another type. Define
whether `created_by` may represent a system/service actor and how that
actor is represented. Ensure `updated_at` and `updated_by` are populated
consistently by the application or database policy.

### Immutability rules

The audit columns do not imply that every transaction row should be
editable. Recommended rules:

- Draft batches, batch-case membership, and assignments may be updated
  while the workflow is open, with audit columns maintained.
- Submitted human reviews should be immutable. A correction should be a
  new/superseding review record with an explicit relationship and audit
  event, not an overwrite of the original submission.
- AI assessment outputs should be immutable. A rerun creates a new AI
  assessment record/attempt.
- Completed comparisons and resolutions should be immutable or revised
  through explicit superseding records, preserving the prior decision.
- Audit events are append-only and should not be updated or deleted by
  normal application workflows.

For immutable records, `updated_at` and `updated_by` may remain null. If
an exceptional administrative correction is allowed, it must be
explicitly authorized and auditable rather than silently changing the
record.

## 11.6 Alternative name for adjudications

Use **`b_human_review_resolutions`** instead of
`b_review_adjudications`.

The word *resolution* describes the business outcome: resolving a
conflict between independent human assessments. It is less tied to a
specific adjudication procedure and remains understandable if the
workflow later includes escalation or additional reviewers.

The table should preserve references to the two independent reviews,
the final label, the resolution rationale, the resolving user, and the
resolution timestamp. A resolution must not overwrite either original
review.

## 11.7 Human review and AI review comparison

A comparison must pin the exact records being compared. It must not
select an arbitrary “latest successful AI result” at display time.

At minimum, `b_review_comparisons` should reference:

- One specific `b_human_reviews` record.
- One specific `b_ai_reviews` record or canonical existing AI evaluation
  record.
- The case version/evidence snapshot used for both, or enough immutable
  references to prove they are aligned.
- The comparison rubric/version and comparison timestamp.

Do not reduce comparison to a single matching/mismatching label. Track
outcome agreement and, where the rubric supports it, evidence selection,
citation grounding, rationale quality, attribution, quantitative
reasoning, contradiction handling, and abstention separately. Use
`NOT_EVALUABLE` when a dimension cannot fairly be compared.

Before creating `b_ai_reviews`, inspect the existing AI evaluation
schema. If it already stores the authoritative AI assessment and exact
attempt metadata, reference that record rather than duplicating the AI
result in a second source of truth. `mst_ai_models` remains the shared
model registry.

## 11.8 Suggested indexes and integrity constraints

Validate against existing indexes and constraints before adding new
ones. Likely requirements include:

- Unique logical case/version key in `b_mst_review_cases`.
- Unique batch/case membership in `b_review_batch_cases`.
- Unique reviewer assignment per batch case and slot, subject to the
  chosen reassignment/history model.
- Unique `assignment_id` in `b_human_reviews` if one submitted review
  per assignment is the approved rule.
- Foreign keys from all label and model references to their master
  tables.
- Unique comparison key for the intended human-review/AI-review pair,
  if duplicate comparisons are not allowed.
- Indexes on foreign keys and common workflow filters (batch, case,
  reviewer, status, model, and evaluation time).
- Constraints preventing a resolution from referencing the same review
  twice and ensuring both reviews belong to the same batch case.

Do not cascade-delete historical reviews, AI assessments, comparisons,
resolutions, or audit events when a case, user, model, or batch is
retired. Use restrictive deletion, soft deactivation, or archival rules
that preserve historical traceability.
