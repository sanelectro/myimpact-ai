## 1. Objective

The existing 14-case benchmark is a useful exploratory baseline. It
covers goal validity, SMART quality, outcome classification, evidence
relevance and sufficiency, contradiction handling, attribution, time
validity, quantitative measurement, citation grounding, and abstention.

This specification improves the existing cases and proposes 20
additional cases to evaluate three separate dimensions:

1.  **Correctness:** Does AI match human-approved decisions?
2.  **Consistency:** Does AI reach stable decisions when the same
    assessment is repeated?
3.  **Robustness:** Does AI behave appropriately when evidence is
    incomplete, irrelevant, contradictory, stale, inaccessible, or
    difficult to attribute?

The original 14 cases and their exploratory results must be preserved.
Proposed new cases and labels are not approved ground truth until
reviewed and approved.

    without human review.

    human review.
-   Define category-specific human labels and an explicit mapping to AI
    output labels.
-   Two independent reviewers must review each case for the governed
    benchmark; disagreements require adjudication under M6.6.2.

    results and release evidence.
-   Do not change the production assessment prompt merely to make the
    benchmark pass. Version prompt changes and evaluate them as separate
    configurations.
-   Present the same complete goal, criterion, evaluation period, and
    evidence context to human reviewers and AI, subject to the existing
    review interface.

### 4.1 Category-specific labels and canonical mapping

The current material uses labels such as `MET`, `IN_PROGRESS`,
`NEEDS_REVIEW`, `NOT_ASSESSED`, `VALID`, `INVALID`, `SMART`,
`NOT_SMART`, `GROUNDED`, and `UNGROUNDED`.

Define a mapping table containing: - Category. - Human-selectable
labels. - AI output labels. - Canonical comparison label, if the system
uses one. - Mapping rule and rationale. - Whether the mapping is
lossless or loses category-specific meaning.

Do not assume `MET` is interchangeable with `VALID`, `SMART`, or
`GROUNDED`. Preserve category-specific labels if that is the established
domain contract.

## 6. Later expansion candidates

The 20 proposed cases are a practical next expansion, not an exhaustive
benchmark. Consider later cases for: - Evidence updated after the
evaluation run. - Deleted or edited source records. - Partial pagination
and result truncation. - No results after a successfully completed
bounded search. - Multiple individually weak evidence items that are
collectively sufficient. - Evidence supporting an outcome but not
employee attribution. - A goal changed midway through the evaluation
period. - A target changed after work began. - A metric whose unit or
denominator changes over time. - A technically true but materially
misleading claim. - A criterion with multiple required subconditions
where only some are satisfied. - A successful outcome achieved through a
different method than originally planned.

Add these only when requirements and expected reviewer decisions can be
defined clearly.

## 8. Human review and adjudication

For the governed benchmark:

1.  Two reviewers independently review every case.
2.  Both receive the same goal, criterion, evaluation period, and
    evidence context.
3.  Reviewers use approved category-specific label options.
4.  Reviewers record concise rationale and, where supported, select
    relevant evidence IDs and citations.
5.  The second reviewer must not see the first review before submitting
    if blind review is required by M6.6.2.
6.  Matching decisions are recorded as agreement.
7.  Different decisions are recorded as disagreement.
8.  An authorized adjudicator resolves each disagreement and records the
    final rationale.
9.  Original reviewer decisions remain immutable.
10. A case is not finalized ground truth until required reviews and
    adjudication are complete.

Report agreement by category as well as overall. Do not hide
category-level disagreement behind one aggregate percentage.

## 9. AI-versus-human evaluation metrics

Calculate correctness separately from repeatability once canonical human
decisions exist.

### 9.1 Classification correctness

For categories with well-defined discrete labels, calculate suitable
measures: - Accuracy. - Per-class precision and recall. - F1 score where
meaningful. - Confusion matrix. - Macro and weighted aggregates where
justified by class distribution.

Do not report a single accuracy figure without class counts and category
breakdowns. If the benchmark is too small or a class has no examples,
report the metric as not evaluable rather than manufacturing a value.

### 9.2 Evidence quality

Measure separately: - Evidence relevance: selected evidence actually
relates to the criterion. - Evidence sufficiency: selected evidence is
sufficient for the decision. - Evidence selection: overlap with
human-approved evidence selections, using a documented method. -
Contradiction handling: relevant contradictory evidence is identified
and represented. - Attribution correctness: claims do not exceed what
evidence establishes.

### 9.3 Citation quality

Measure: - Citation presence where required. - Citation resolves to the
referenced evidence. - Citation supports the specific claim it
accompanies. - Citation does not omit material contradictory context.

A citation that exists is not necessarily a grounded citation.

### 9.4 Abstention quality

Distinguish: - Correct abstention when information is insufficient. -
Incorrect abstention when sufficient evidence supports a decision. -
Failure to abstain when evidence is insufficient. - Appropriate
qualified/partial assessment where the contract allows it.

### 9.5 Repeatability

Retain M6.6.4 repeatability metrics separately: - Label consistency. -
Evidence-selection consistency. - Citation-set consistency. - Abstention
consistency. - Exact normalized-output consistency.

Document formula, comparison unit, denominator, and treatment of failed
attempts for every metric. Verify the implementation's formula before
relying on the existing `57.1%` repeatability figure.
